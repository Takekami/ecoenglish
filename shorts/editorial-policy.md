# Econenglish Shorts: editorial direction

Agreed 2026-10-29 after reviewing the first previews.

## Promise

知っている経済用語を、使える英語に。

The primary outcome is recognizing, hearing and saying an English word or phrase.
Economic knowledge is context for choosing useful vocabulary, not the lesson's
assessment target. Do not ask a viewer to interpret inflation statistics, predict
markets, or explain central-bank policy to complete a Short.

## Expression level

Aim at intermediate Japanese learners, broadly the Podcast's B1–B2 audience.
This is an editorial target, not a certified CEFR rating for individual phrases.
Prefer 2–6-word economic/business collocations, verb+noun patterns and fixed
expressions: familiar words combined in a way learners may struggle to produce.
Choose usefulness and transfer to other news over rarity. Bare common nouns such
as `exports` are insufficient alone; adding an obvious adjective solely to reach
two words is also insufficient. Avoid obscure terminology and brand names.
The deterministic gate rejects single-word selections; the independent reviewer
assesses collocation usefulness and contextual difficulty. If the Script has no
suitable expression, skip the Short rather than rewriting the Script to force it.

## External usage and transfer check

Before video production, actually consult a dictionary or a company's official
announcement. Record publisher, title, URL, a short excerpt containing the same
expression, and the date checked. Record why the expression transfers to other
companies, sectors or situations, with at least two reuse contexts. A published
Podcast sentence alone is not sufficient evidence of natural external usage.

The generation call cannot browse and must not invent sources or certify itself.
Keep the primary-source review outside its output, tied to the exact expression
and script hash. Conversion/rendering reject a missing or mismatched record before
paid speech generation. The record is an editorial check, not an automatic page
fetch or a frequency measurement. Do not describe an attested expression as
high-frequency, the most common, or the only correct wording without further
evidence. If naturalness or transfer remains uncertain, hold or skip the Short.

## Standard flow

1. Introduce a familiar Japanese economic term and offer one possible English expression.
2. Show and pronounce that expression; avoid framing it as the only correct answer.
3. Play one short original example containing the same expression unchanged.
4. Give its natural Japanese translation.
5. Add useful language knowledge in an explicitly introduced application stage.
6. Replay the original example and point to the free learning materials.

For example, introduce `a rate cut` and use `The central bank announced a rate
cut.` Do not switch to `cut interest rates` in its place. Both expressions can
be valid, but each Short now teaches one expression consistently. Sentence-initial
capitalization may change; articles, noun/verb form and wording must otherwise
remain the same. The renderer enforces full-expression matching before paid audio
generation. Additional expressions are welcome after that example when clearly
introduced as an application, e.g. `ここからは、動詞を使った言い方です`.
The screen labels this `応用：動詞で言うなら` and retains the original noun phrase
for comparison. Collocations may use a different explicit label. Such useful
extensions must not silently substitute for the first expression in its example.

Keep Japanese speech conversational; subtitles may be shorter. Pronounce English
terms with the English voice rather than adding long katakana explanations.
Use either approved Japanese voice, Marin or Cedar, alternating across videos.
Keep the same Japanese voice for all scenes of a given video.

## Trust

Use neutral questions and concrete learning value. No fear, shame, score
guarantees, market predictions or investment advice. Examples are labeled as
original exercises and must not imply that an invented event actually occurred.
Reference labels identify background reading, not endorsements or the source of
a fabricated quotation. Reference links belong in the metadata draft. Brief
semantic distinctions may be included only when essential to a correct English
translation. Broader economic background belongs in an optional blog article.

These are local previews. No channel modification, upload, publication or daily
schedule is enabled by the preview scripts.
